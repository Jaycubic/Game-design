# Clearwater Run — one crew against the pollution (design, round 3)

Status: **built** 2026-11-16 · **approved** (with the owner's refinements: lock popup, ▲ to skip, several fixers first-come-first-served, catch-up current, suggested fixer)

> **Update after playtesting (2026-11-16):** the name "Murk" was dropped from every screen and document because it
> confused people. The health bar is plain **River health**; the grumpy smog cloud over open pipes stays as a
> graphic. A **Quit** button (✕ beside the timer, tap twice) was added to runs.

## Why

Playtesting by the owner showed that the gate code is a solo task dressed up as teamwork: one player knows the
answer and dictates it, and nothing has to be worked out. With 2–4 players split into rival crews, the game also
leans on competition between friends. The professor asked for no violence; competition is allowed but not
required, and bluffing is optional.

This round makes Clearwater Run a **co-operative game: everyone in the room is one crew, playing against the
pollution**, and gives every player a piece of each pipe's lock so the crew can only fix it together.

## Goals

1. **One crew, always.** 1–4 players are one team. Nobody plays against a friend.
2. **A clear opponent:** the pollution itself, given a face ("Murk"), so there is still something to beat.
3. **Real teamwork at pipes:** each player holds one secret key; the lock needs everyone's key.
4. **Team Endless** for 1–4 players, chasing a crew high score.
5. Keep what works: lanes, rivers, stars, missions, ranks, Seeds, the River Director, exactly two player-chosen
   rule changes per run.

## Non-goals

- **No bluffing.** No fake signals and no tricks from the game. Signals are always honest.
- No new rivers or currency; no change to the Seeds economy beyond what's below.
- No player-vs-player modes. (Comparing crew scores on a leaderboard is possible later; not in this round.)

## 1. One crew

- Every player in a run is in **crew 0**. With 3–4 players there are no longer two crews.
- The crew shares one score, one River Health bar and one result.
- **Winning = beating Murk:** the run is a win when the river is **revived** (River Health 60 or more at the end).
  It is a loss when the river **chokes** (River Health reaches 0). In between, the run is "complete" and the score
  still counts.
- Seeds: the win bonus is paid for a revived river (first 3 a day, as now).
- The River Director can now adapt during every run, because there is only ever one crew.

## 2. Murk, the pollution

Murk is the face of the pollution: a **smoggy brown cloud with a grumpy face**. It is friendly-silly, not scary,
and never attacks anyone.

- It hangs over every open sewage pipe and puffs murk into the water. When the pipe is plugged, Murk shrinks away
  ("Bleh!") and disappears.
- The River Health bar becomes a tug-of-war: the crew's side (clean, blue) against Murk's side (brown). The more
  the river is polluted, the further Murk's side pushes in.
- Results say it in words: "You beat Murk! The river is alive." / "Murk took the river this time."
- In text, everything bad on the river is Murk's doing (sewage, dumped plastic, debris waves), so young players
  get a simple story: **we clean, Murk pollutes, we stop Murk at the pipes.**

## 3. The pipe lock: everyone holds a key

### What players see

1. **Keys are handed out.** When the leading boat is about 70 m from an open pipe, every player privately sees
   **their own key** for 3 seconds: "Your key: 🐟 Fish — remember it!". Each player in the crew gets a different
   symbol from the six. Nobody else sees your key. Then it hides.
2. **A fixer calls the crew.** A player beside the pipe presses **Action**. That is the call for help: their boat
   stops and a small **lock popup** opens with **one slot per player, in seat order** (P1, P2, P3, P4), each in that
   player's colour and name, for example: 🔵 Ben · 🟡 You · 🟠 Asha.
3. **Teammates answer.** Every other player instantly gets a loud prompt: "Ben is fixing a pipe — press your key!"
   (a sound, and a buzz on phones). They press the number of the symbol they were shown. Their key **appears in
   their slot in the popup** (and pops up over their boat in their colour). Distance doesn't matter.
4. **The fixer enters the lock.** The fixer types the slots **left to right as the popup shows them**: their own
   key from memory, the others as they arrive. Order is never a guessing game; the popup always shows it.
5. **One timer for the whole lock: 12 seconds.**
6. **Right keys:** the boat stays put 1 second while the crew member fixes the pipe (as now), the river heals by
   10, the multiplier rises, Murk shrinks away.
   **A wrong key, or a teammate not answering in time:** the fix fails and **Murk surges** (River Health −5 at
   once); the pipe keeps pouring, and the next attempt hands out **new keys**.
7. **Skip.** A fixer can leave the lock at any time by pressing **▲ (hop)**: the lock closes and the boat sails on.
   This is how a crew that all stopped can leave one person behind to finish the job.

### More than one fixer at the same pipe

Allowed, and it opens up strategy.
- Anyone beside the pipe can press Action and open their own popup.
- Every key a teammate presses goes into **all** open popups for that pipe at once (one press helps everyone; a
  fixer's own key, typed into their popup, also fills their slot in the other fixers' popups).
- **First come, first served:** the first fixer to enter the full set correctly fixes the pipe. The other popups
  close with "Fixed by Asha!".
- Crews can choose: everyone stops for a lightning-fast fix, or one fixer stays behind (the others press ▲ to
  sail on) while the rest keep collecting. Some pipes sit in awkward spots (right after a log row, among Gorge
  currents), so the choice matters.

### Catching up after a fix

Stopping must never strand a player. A boat more than about **25 m behind the crew's front boat** sails **15 %
faster** (a "catch-up current") until it rejoins. Fixing costs a little time and plastic, never the run.

### Everyone gets a turn

- Boats start spread across lanes and pipes alternate between the left and right banks, so different players end
  up nearest different pipes.
- **Suggested fixer:** each pipe's marker glows in the colour of the player who has fixed the fewest pipes so far
  this run ("Asha's turn?"). It's a hint; anyone can still fix it.
- Results show **pipes fixed per player**, and a new mission: "Fix a pipe yourself".

### Why it's a puzzle and teamwork

- **Nobody can fix a pipe alone**: the lock needs a key that only each teammate has.
- Each player must **remember** their key, **notice** when a teammate calls, and **answer** in time, even while
  racing ahead.
- Leaving a teammate behind is a choice with a cost: if you don't answer, Murk surges.

### Solo

With one player there is no one to share with, so solo keeps the **memory version**: the 3-symbol code shows in
order for 2 seconds, then hides as ? ? ?, and you enter it from memory (8 seconds). ▲ skips here too.

### Edge cases

- A teammate who is disconnected or "away": their slot is filled automatically, so a lock is never impossible.
- The tutorial's single pipe uses the solo memory version.

## 4. The two rule changes

Still **exactly two per run, both chosen by players**.

- **Twist (about 1:03):** the crew votes, as now. Ties and no votes are settled by a visible spin.
- **Finale (about 2:20):** the crew now **votes** too (there is no trailing crew). Same two options: Second Wind
  (boost and extra plastic for the crew for 20 s) or Clean Sweep (open pipes pour at half rate). Ties spin.

The negotiation now happens inside the crew: "Second Wind gets us points, but the river is at 40 — Clean Sweep?"

## 5. Team Endless (1–4 players)

- Available from the hub for solo play **and** from a crew room (the host starts it).
- **Shared lives:** the crew has **3 lives**. Every capsize uses one (and still spills plastic). The run ends when
  the lives are gone or the river chokes. Solo Endless keeps "one capsize ends it" (one life).
- The boats speed up 2 % every 15 s; two player-chosen rule changes at 60 s and 150 s; pipe locks as in §3.
- **Score = metres + 5 × plastic** (whole crew). Each player's best Endless score per river is kept, as now.
- Pays like practice (half Seeds). Counts for missions. No stars. Doesn't change the skill rating.

## 6. What changes elsewhere

| Area | Change |
|---|---|
| Lobby | Says "Your crew: 1–4 players". No crew split. |
| HUD | One crew score; River Health shown as the crew vs Murk; the pipe card and marker stay. |
| Results | "You beat Murk!" / "Murk took the river"; crew score, stars, missions, rank. |
| Missions | "Win a crew race" becomes "Beat Murk (finish with the river revived)". |
| Signals | Same six symbols; now used to send your key. No fixed meaning needed. |
| Tutorial | The pipe step shows the memory version and a short line: "With friends, everyone holds one key." |
| How to play / Rule Book / guide | Rewritten for one crew, Murk, keys and team Endless; the "Fair play" table drops crew-vs-crew rows. |
| Server | Run creation puts every seat in crew 0; Endless allowed with more than one player. |

## 7. Build order

1. One crew everywhere (core, results, Seeds, missions, lobby, HUD text), the Finale vote and the catch-up current.
2. The key lock (core, snapshot per viewer, popup and answer prompt, several fixers first-come-first-served, ▲ to
   skip, Murk surge on failure, auto-fill for away players), the catch-up current and the suggested fixer, with tests.
3. Murk: the cloud over open pipes, the tug-of-war health bar, result wording.
4. Team Endless with shared lives.
5. Rule Book, beginner guide, How to play, API, design doc.

Each step is tested (core unit tests, server tests, a browser playthrough) and deployed when it passes.

## Builder's calls (adjustable after playtesting)

- Key reveal distance (70 m) and time (3 s).
- Lock time (12 s; solo 8 s).
- Slots in seat order; keys are all different within a crew.
- Murk surge on a failed lock: −5 River Health.
- Catch-up current: +15 % speed when more than 25 m behind the front boat.
- 3 shared lives in team Endless.
- Murk's look (a cloud with a face, made in code like the other props).
